# Parallel and Perpendicular Classroom Lesson (beta)

Student route `/apps/beta/parallel-perpendicular-lesson`; teacher route adds `/teacher`. The existing `/apps/beta/write-linear-parallel-perpendicular` and original classroom practice are preserved and linked as optional review. Only the Beta Apps directory lists the new activity. No course lesson links are added.

The authoritative twelve fixed records in `src/lib/parallel-lesson.ts` follow eight stable slide numbers: P1; P2a/P2b/P2c; P3; P4; T5; T6a/T6b/T6c; T7; T8. All match the teacher’s supplied redesign, including the common starting point (6,-2), exact intercepts -14/3 and 11/2, and independent intercepts 7/3 and -3/2. No horizontal or vertical given lines are used. Teacher and student views consume the same data, with independent local state and no live student/teacher connection.

Student mode collects first/last/hour, fixes the givens while checking, and unlocks Next only on a correct equation. Slides 2/6 require separate correct blue/new slope checks before the final field, and show 0–3 complete based on unique solved records. Slope mistakes mark first-try status separately; they never advance a three-problem checkpoint. Slope arrows appear after the new slope is checked. Their small direction segment does not expose the whole exact red line.

Drawing is freehand and ungraded on Slides 1/2/5/6. Slides 3/7 and the independent tries have no sketch capture. Every readable y= linear entry is graphed, even if its form needs expansion. Valid wrong equations are dashed red, editable, and labeled Your line, with an overlap notice when identical to the blue line. Equation attempts increment only for readable entries in the requested expanded form. Format reminders do not consume attempts. Success draws a solid red line, a brief burst at the gold point, a solved-attempt label and Next in the same button position. The keypad remains in place but disables on success to avoid a large layout jump. Checks compare coefficient values and the target point independently. Feedback identifies a correct slope/missed point (including the actual y-value), a correct point/wrong relationship, or both failed conditions. Equivalent fractions are accepted.

Teacher presentation bypasses name/score flow and allows navigation to every problem at any time. Four manual reveals give the original slope, new slope relationship, point-slope substitution, and distribution/final equation with the point check. The graph stays large and pinned, with the givens repeated above it; reveal controls stay accessible. A quarter-turn arrow on Slide 5 maps right 3/up 2 to right 2/down 3. A right-angle marker appears for perpendicular submitted lines. Reduced motion uses the final arrow direction and a static point highlight.

Completion of all twelve problems earns 10/10; first-try successes and equation attempts are reported separately rather than preventing corrected work from earning completion. Two required confidence/difficulty reflections precede automatic named local image preparation. The image includes local date/time/zone and all problem IDs/attempts. Native image actions provide Photos saving when offered; no server score submission or browser persistence is used. Spanish copy and feedback are local. The matching notes PDF remains English.

PPL resume codes encode version, an initials/hour hash, solved/missed-slope flags, equation attempts, completion minute and checksum. Only completed-problem checkpoints are offered. On restore, finished problems are reconstructed and the next unfinished problem begins with fresh input/sketch; mathematical attempt/first-try history is retained. Codes are not authentication or encryption. Teacher mode creates neither scores nor result cards.

`public/handouts/parallel-perpendicular-classroom-notes.pdf` is an original two-page handout generated by `scripts/create-parallel-lesson-notes.py`. Front: parallel; back: perpendicular. All twelve IDs, prompts, grid/work areas, definitions and four algebra steps align with the widget. Print duplex, long-edge binding. This generated notes sheet is not a course assessment or an uploaded source-folder document.
